Section: The Space
One bedroom. One bathroom with a deep soaking tub. A kitchen stocked before you arrive. A living area that faces the water. A fireplace for the cold nights — and on the East Coast, there are cold nights.
The retreat sleeps two. It is not designed for more.
Section: The Architecture
The brief was simple: disappear into the land, open to the water. Raked concrete ceilings follow the fall of the hill. Blackwood joinery runs through the interior. The fireplace anchors the living space. Glazing on the north and east faces tracks the light from morning through to dusk. Nothing is decorative. Every material was chosen because it belongs here.
Section: Off the Grid
The retreat runs entirely on solar power and harvested rainwater. There is no mains connection. The system is designed to run quietly in the background — you will not notice it unless the sky stays dark for days, at which point the backup generator takes over. Water is tank water.

Entry 1
Date: March 2025
Title: What the tide leaves
We found a cluster of Neptune's necklace near the southern rocks at low tide last week. It had been a calm few days — the bay flat, the wind offshore. The kind of morning where you walk further than you planned.
There's a particular quality to the light here in autumn. It arrives slowly, stays low, and makes everything look considered. The water holds a colour that doesn't have a name.
We've been talking to a local guide about putting together a coastal foraging session for guests. She knows this stretch of coast better than anyone. More soon.

Entry 2
Date: June 2025
Title: Cold nights, good fire
Winter on the East Coast is not brutal, but it's honest. The mornings are still and sharp. The bay turns a deeper green. The firewood stack outside the retreat earns its place.
We've been stocking the larder with heavier provisions through winter — Wild Hives honey in generous quantities, Barkmill's rye loaf, warmer things. Milton Vineyard's Pinot Noir makes sense in a way it doesn't in February.
There's something about being off-grid in winter that feels more intentional than in summer. The solar does its work quietly. The rain fills the tanks. The system holds.

Entry 3
Date: August 2025
Title: On the off-grid question
We get asked about the off-grid setup a lot. People want to know if it's reliable, whether the power drops out, what happens when it's cloudy for a week.
The honest answer: the system is overbuilt on purpose. Solar panels, battery bank, automatic backup generator. The sky can stay dark for days. The system holds.
The reason we run this way isn't a marketing decision. The property sits in a place that asks something of you. Off-grid felt like the right response to that. The rainwater, the septic, the solar — they're all part of the same logic. You don't extract from a place like this without noticing.

Page intro:
The questions we get asked most. If yours isn't here, write to us.

THE STAY
How many guests does the retreat accommodate?
Two. Rocky Hills is designed for couples or pairs travelling together. It is not suitable for groups of more than two.
What is the minimum stay?
Two nights. We recommend three or more to settle in properly.
What time is check-in and check-out?
Check-in from 3pm. Check-out by 10am. Early check-in or late check-out may be possible — ask us.
Is the retreat suitable for children?
Rocky Hills is designed for adults. We don't accommodate children.
Can we bring a pet?
No pets. The property sits in habitat for native wildlife.

THE FOOD
Do you provide meals?
All provisions and food packages are delivered to the retreat with full recipes and preparation notes. We like to leave you in peace. Order ahead — we'll have everything ready on arrival.
Can we add provisions after booking?
Yes. Contact us at least 48 hours before arrival to add provisions, experiences, or cellar selections.
Do you cater for dietary requirements?
Most provisions can be adapted. Let us know when you book and we'll do our best.

THE PROPERTY
Is the property really off-grid?
Yes. Solar panels and battery storage supply all power. Water is harvested rainwater. Waste is managed on-site via septic. There is no mains connection of any kind.
What happens if the power goes out?
The system includes an automatic backup generator that activates if the battery drops below a safe level.
Is there WiFi?
Yes. Satellite internet is available throughout the retreat. It is not fast by city standards, but it is reliable.
Is there phone reception?
Patchy. Telstra performs best in the area. Consider this part of the experience.
How far is the nearest town?
Swansea is a short drive. It has a supermarket, a bottle shop, cafes, and a bakery.

THE ECO CREDENTIALS
What does Sustainable Tourism Certified mean?
Rocky Hills holds Sustainable Tourism Certification with Ecotourism Australia — the national body for responsible tourism. The certification covers environmental management, energy, water, waste, community, and cultural practices. We're assessed and audited. It's not self-declared.
Are you carbon neutral?
Not yet. We're working toward it. The off-grid infrastructure means our operational emissions are very low — but we're being careful about what we claim and when we claim it.
Why off-grid?
Because the property sits on country that asks something of you. Off-grid felt like the right response to that. The solar, the rainwater, the septic — they're all part of the same logic. You don't extract from a place like this without noticing.
